# HazRob 0.10.2 / HR069 — MQ76 Qualification and Drift Workflow

Date: 2026-09-29
Branch: `release/hazrob-0.10.2`

## Purpose

This document locks the implementation boundary added after the HR069 analytical research audit. The objective is to use all 76 MQDataScience models as research candidates without allowing model fit quality to bypass HazRob calibration, open-set, domain, mixture or M4 reportability gates.

## 1. Complete MQ76 research path

1. Export a controlled HazRob lab trace with `time_s,response` and, when available, `temperature_c,rh_pct`.
2. Run `tools/hazrob_mq76_upstream.py`.
3. The bridge verifies the pinned upstream `PredictData.py` Git blob and executes the complete 76-model catalogue.
4. Preserve every model success/failure and CV result. The highest-CV-R2 model is a research ranking only.
5. Pass the research JSON through `tools/qualify_mq76_result.py` with explicit sensor, chemical, sampling-profile, validity-domain and independent held-out metadata.
6. The qualifier verifies that all 76 model records came from the pinned upstream authority and emits either `RESEARCH_ONLY` or `QUALIFICATION_CANDIDATE`.
7. The qualifier always emits `activation_eligible=false` and `m4_granted=false`. Runtime activation remains a separate scientific review and reference-gas validation action.

## 2. Why all 76 are retained but not all are concentration curves

The upstream catalogue contains environmental/time-response, classical/nonlinear curve, temporal/state-space and machine-learning families. Many are useful for stabilization, drift, response/recovery dynamics, nuisance-variable modelling and research ranking. It is not scientifically valid to reinterpret ARIMA, Holt-Winters, Kalman or other time-series forecasts as universal Rs/R0-to-ppm calibration equations.

The role registry therefore keeps all 76 available while preserving task-specific scientific semantics.

## 3. Physical heater qualification

`HazRobHardwareProfile` is now the runtime authority for whether the installed hardware can execute the manufacturer heater protocol.

HR069 uses one common 5 V MQ heater rail. Therefore:

- MQ-2, MQ-3, MQ-4, MQ-5, MQ-8 and MQ-135 are heater-qualified for their continuous-5 V heater requirement, subject to the remaining manufacturer/domain/calibration gates.
- MQ-7 and MQ-9 remain heater-unqualified because their 5.0 V high / 1.5 V low cyclic heater protocol cannot be generated by the installed common rail.

`AnalyticsSnapshot` now derives the heater-validity vector from the hardware profile rather than from an unexplained literal boolean array. The analytical JSON records the hardware authority, cyclic-driver state and valid/invalid channels.

This is a fail-closed software safeguard. It does not replace the future physical installation and qualification of a dedicated MQ-7/MQ-9 heater driver.

## 4. Longitudinal drift surveillance

`ChemCoreAnchorDriftMonitor` adds non-destructive longitudinal surveillance of clean-air/reference anchors in log10 response space.

The monitor:

- requires multiple independent anchor sessions per channel;
- computes the median multiplicative log shift and robust median absolute deviation;
- classifies the supplied policy as `STABLE`, `WATCH`, `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED`, or `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`;
- never changes R0 automatically;
- never overwrites an M4 calibration;
- requires an explicit caller-supplied policy and source, so literature/example thresholds cannot silently become device acceptance limits.

A `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` result means the existing calibration remains historical evidence and a new calibration revision must be created and independently qualified.

## 5. Required evidence before a model can progress

For any sensor/analyte model intended to advance beyond research ranking, retain at minimum:

- physical sensor/module identity;
- exact HazRob app/firmware/hardware profile;
- chemical/reference-gas identity and preparation traceability;
- concentration levels and independent replicates;
- sampling/chamber profile;
- temperature/RH domain and relevant environmental context;
- training, validation and blind/session-holdout experiment counts;
- held-out error metrics in the relevant response/concentration domain;
- residual and extrapolation checks;
- drift/temporal split performance;
- uncertainty/coverage evidence;
- model/source provenance and artifact hash;
- M4 reference-gas validation status.
